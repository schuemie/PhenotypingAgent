Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) is a unipolar mood disorder characterized by one or more discrete Major Depressive Episodes, each defined by a minimum two-week period during which the individual experiences either persistent depressed mood or markedly diminished interest or pleasure in nearly all activities, accompanied by at least four additional neurovegetative, cognitive, or somatic symptoms from the DSM-5 criterion set â€” including significant weight or appetite change, insomnia or hypersomnia, psychomotor agitation or retardation, fatigue or loss of energy, feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt, diminished concentration or indecisiveness, and recurrent thoughts of death or suicidal ideation. The episode must cause clinically significant distress or functional impairment and must not be attributable to the physiological effects of a substance or another medical condition. This phenotype encompasses presentations across the full severity spectrum (mild, moderate, and severe) but explicitly excludes depressive episodes occurring within the context of Bipolar I or Bipolar II Disorder, substance-induced depressive disorder, depressive disorder due to a general medical condition, Persistent Depressive Disorder (dysthymia), adjustment disorder with depressed mood, and bereavement-related low mood that does not satisfy the full MDE symptom threshold and duration requirement.
